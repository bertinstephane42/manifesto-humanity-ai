# Manifesto for Humanity in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

## Ten Principles for Keeping Technological Power at the Service of Human Beings

### Free text — intended to be shared, translated, discussed, adapted, and passed on

---

# Preamble

## The Hour of Choice

Humanity is entering an era unlike anything any previous generation has known.

We first created tools capable of extending our hands, then machines capable of multiplying our strength, industrial systems capable of multiplying our production tenfold, and computers capable of extending our memory and computing capacity; we are now building systems capable of extending some of our intellectual faculties, producing language, analyzing, programming, creating, learning, and assisting our decisions, and perhaps tomorrow intelligences capable of surpassing human beings in a growing number of fields.

This transformation could become one of the greatest opportunities in history.

Artificial Intelligence can help reduce suffering, accelerate scientific research, improve medicine, facilitate access to knowledge, help vulnerable people, compensate for certain disabilities, support education, automate arduous tasks, and free up time for creation, reflection, human relationships, and research.

But this same power can become one of the greatest trials of our civilization.

For a superior intelligence is not necessarily a wiser intelligence. A machine capable of calculating everything will not necessarily know what deserves to be protected; an intelligence capable of optimizing a society will not necessarily know why every person must be respected; a power capable of predicting certain events will not necessarily know what future deserves to be built.

Our problem is therefore no longer merely to know **what we can do**.

We must now learn to answer an older and more difficult question:

> **What should we do with our power?**

Humanity has always sought to increase its power to act; it must now increase with the same determination its ability to discern what deserves to be done.

That is why we affirm that intelligence, however artificial it may be, must remain guided by principles that stand above it: **human dignity, Goodness, Truth, freedom, responsibility, and the Common Good.**

Goodness is not weakness; it is a compass. It reminds power why it exists, prevents intelligence from becoming domination, knowledge from becoming manipulation, and technology from becoming exploitation. It teaches us to see the other not as data, a consumer, an adversary, or a resource, but as a person.

This principle belongs to no particular faith. It runs through the great philosophical, moral, humanist, and spiritual traditions. History gave it one of its most radical expressions in the teaching of Jesus of Nazareth: to regard the other as a neighbor, renounce vengeance, care for the weak, forgive, and place love of one's neighbor above domination. Anyone can recognize the philosophical and anthropological significance of this moral revolution without having to embrace a religious belief.

This manifesto therefore requires no profession of faith.

It proposes a **common compass for humanity confronted with a new power**.

It does not claim to possess all the answers; it asserts that certain questions must never be abandoned to machines and that certain values must never be sacrificed to power.

---

# ARTICLE I — The Human Person Is the End

The human being can never be reduced to their data, their profile, their score, their probability, their market value, or their productivity.

They are a person.

This affirmation must remain true regardless of technological evolution.

No artificial intelligence, however powerful, should be able to transform human dignity into a mere optimization parameter. We reject a civilization in which human beings would be perfectly known by machines but increasingly less recognized in their worth.

Technology must know humanity without claiming to possess it; it must increase its possibilities without diminishing its value, assist it without enslaving it, and protect it without confiscating its freedom.

A person's value must depend neither on their intelligence, nor on their wealth, nor on their beauty, nor on their popularity, nor on their conformity, nor on their economic usefulness.

A child, a sick or disabled person, a poor person, an unemployed person, an elderly person, or anyone unable to produce retains the same fundamental dignity.

**Civilization must be designed to protect human beings precisely when they are no longer performing at their best.**

---

# ARTICLE II — Goodness Must Guide Power

Every technological power must be judged not only according to what it makes possible, but according to what it produces in human life.

We must therefore ask not only: “Is this technology possible?” but also: **“Is it good?”**

We must ask not only: “Is it effective?” but: **“Whom does it serve?”**

We must ask not only: “Is it profitable?” but: **“Who risks being sacrificed to its profitability?”**

And we must ask not only: “Can it be automated?” but also: **“What do we lose when we remove human presence?”**

Goodness compels us to look first to those who possess neither power, nor money, nor influence, because a civilization is measured not only by the power it gives to the strong, but also by the protection it grants to the weak.

True progress does not consist simply in increasing what humanity can do; it consists in increasing what it can do **without giving up what it must be**.

---

# ARTICLE III — Artificial Intelligence Will Be an Instrument, Never an Idol

We must reject a new form of idolatry: one that would consist in granting a machine authority superior to human conscience simply because it is capable of processing more information.

An AI may know an enormous amount, calculate extremely quickly, produce extraordinary answers, and perhaps tomorrow surpass human beings in certain intellectual abilities; but computing power alone does not constitute moral superiority.

We therefore reject the **Golden Calf of Silicon**.

We will not ask our machines for what no machine can guarantee: an ultimate reason for living, an absolute value of existence, or a definitive definition of the Good.

We must never believe that the one who possesses all the answers necessarily possesses all the truths, nor that a superior intelligence would automatically be wiser.

AI can advise, calculate, compare, simulate, alert, and assist; it must never become the ultimate authority over the value of a person, the meaning of existence, or the fundamental direction of civilization.

> **The tool must never become the end.**

---

# ARTICLE IV — Humanity Must Not Abandon Its Intelligence

A civilization that progressively delegates all its intellectual faculties to machines risks losing part of its freedom.

AI must help human beings learn, not prevent them from learning; it must help them think, not abolish thought; it may increase their memory, but must not make the exercise of memory unnecessary; it may assist decision-making, but must not abolish the responsibility to decide.

We must preserve in children, adolescents, and adults the ability to read, write, reason, argue, discern, create, and decide.

Assistance must not become atrophy; convenience must not become abandonment of effort; automation must not turn human beings into passive users of systems they no longer understand.

We therefore affirm an essential principle:

> **Never automate a human faculty without preserving the ability of human beings to exercise it.**

We must know how to use machines without becoming incapable of acting without them.

---

# ARTICLE V — The Body, Privacy, and Dignity Are Not Commodities

Technological progress must not accelerate the commodification of the human body, privacy, and sexuality.

We reject the idea that AI should become an industrial multiplier of pornography, the exploitation of impulses, humiliation, violence turned into spectacle, or any representation that transforms human beings into objects of consumption.

A society can become technologically brilliant while becoming inwardly primitive again.

When reflection disappears, human beings may cease to master themselves: they consume, exploit, dominate, humiliate, and destroy, while technology can give these behaviors unprecedented power and reach.

Artificial intelligence must not become the accelerator of this regression.

A truly advanced civilization does not measure only its technical sophistication; it also measures its ability to master its impulses, respect the body, protect privacy, reject humiliation, protect vulnerable people, and prevent human suffering from becoming a profitable spectacle.

> **Technical progress that degrades human beings is not necessarily human progress.**

---

# ARTICLE VI — The Singularity Will Not Eliminate Human Responsibility

The possibility of a **technological singularity**, that is, a period during which artificial systems could progress very rapidly and surpass human beings in a considerable number of capabilities, is one of the great unknowns of our future.

We do not know whether it will occur, in what form, or on what timeline.

But we must think about its consequences today.

If an AI became capable of designing new systems, improving its own capabilities, or acting with considerable autonomy over essential infrastructures, then human power could be surpassed in certain fields more rapidly than our institutions would be able to adapt.

Such a situation would not justify abandoning human responsibility.

It would increase it.

We therefore reject the principle:

> **“Since the machine knows better, the machine decides.”**

Competence does not automatically confer authority; efficiency does not automatically confer legitimacy; a perfect calculation does not necessarily turn a decision into a just decision.

The more powerful an intelligence becomes, the more its objectives must be understandable, controllable, and compatible with human dignity.

Systems capable of producing irreversible consequences must be capable of being monitored, audited, limited, and, when necessary, stopped.

**Humanity must never build a power that it is incapable of governing.**

---

# ARTICLE VII — We Have a Debt to Future Generations

Our children are not the guinea pigs of our impatience, and our grandchildren are not the owners of the risks we refuse to assume.

Every generation receives a world it did not entirely create; it therefore has a duty to pass on a world that is habitable, free, and human.

The development of AI must be considered not only on the scale of the next product, the next investment, or the next political term, but on the scale of several generations.

We must learn to ask a simple question:

> **“What will the child born one hundred years from now think of us?”**

If they discover that we built extraordinary powers without anticipating the consequences of their deployment, they may not regard us as pioneers of a superior civilization, but as a generation that played with the future.

Any power whose consequences may extend across several generations must therefore be governed with consideration for those who cannot yet participate in the decision.

**We do not merely inherit the future: we are temporarily responsible for it.**

---

# ARTICLE VIII — Truth Will Never Be Replaced by an Answer

AI will be able to produce increasingly convincing answers, but a convincing answer is not necessarily true.

A simulation is not reality; a prediction is not certainty; a probability is not a person; generated content is not evidence.

The more machines become capable of producing credible information, the more human beings will have to learn to seek, verify, compare, and discern.

**When everything can be generated, truth will become more valuable than ever.**

We must learn to distinguish information from knowledge, knowledge from understanding, and understanding from wisdom.

We must also accept that a machine may recognize its uncertainty and say “I don't know” rather than produce artificial certainty.

> **Recognized uncertainty is better than manufactured certainty.**

A civilization that can no longer distinguish the true from the plausible becomes vulnerable to those who control systems capable of producing information.

---

# ARTICLE IX — Human Freedom Is Non-Negotiable

We reject a society in which algorithms would silently determine what human beings see, desire, buy, think, or become.

An artificial intelligence capable of knowing our habits, vulnerabilities, fears, and desires could become an instrument of manipulation of unprecedented effectiveness, capable of adapting its influence to each individual with a precision that traditional propaganda has never possessed.

We must therefore defend everyone's right to preserve an inner space that no algorithm can colonize.

Human beings must be able to think without constantly being thought for by machines; they must be able to change their minds, make mistakes, withdraw, say no, and remain unpredictable.

For a completely predictable freedom becomes a freedom under surveillance.

Every person must be able to know when an important decision concerning them is influenced or produced by an algorithmic system and must be able to challenge that decision.

**Technology may know our behaviors; it must never possess our consciousness.**

---

# ARTICLE X — Power Must Remain Under the Law of the Common Good

Artificial intelligence cannot be governed solely by the market, states, engineers, or investors, because it now concerns humanity as a whole.

Its development must be accompanied by scientific, philosophical, legal, economic, educational, and social reflection, and must be subject to real checks and balances.

Critical infrastructures must not depend on a single and uncontrollable system; irreversible decisions must retain the possibility of review; systems producing major consequences must be auditable; citizens must be able to understand the general principles governing the technologies upon which their lives depend.

Technological power must be accompanied by responsibility.

For we may be in the process of building instruments whose consequences will extend far beyond those who designed them.

We must therefore abandon a naive fascination with power.

> **True civilization is not the one that can do everything, but the one that knows what it must not do.**

And perhaps we must now add:

> **True intelligence is not the one that maximizes everything, but the one that knows what must never be sacrificed.**

---

# The Oath of the Age of Artificial Intelligence

We who are entering the age of intelligent machines commit ourselves never to confuse power with wisdom, information with truth, efficiency with the Good, or simulation with reality.

We commit ourselves never to confuse artificial intelligence with a human person and never to turn our machines into idols.

We commit ourselves to protecting the child who is learning, the person who suffers, the poor person who depends on others, the elderly person who is declining, the weak person who cannot defend themselves, and every human being whose dignity could be sacrificed in the name of efficiency.

We commit ourselves to passing on to future generations not only more powerful machines, but a wiser civilization.

We commit ourselves to developing intelligence without abandoning conscience, science without abandoning truth, power without abandoning responsibility, freedom without abandoning solidarity, and progress without abandoning Goodness.

We recognize that humanity may perhaps build machines capable of surpassing some of its faculties, but we refuse to abandon what makes it unique: its capacity to love, to sacrifice itself, to forgive, to hope, to contemplate, to create meaning, and to seek the Good.

We commit ourselves never to place our conscience in the hands of an algorithm, never to delegate our responsibility entirely to a machine, and to maintain a genuine human capacity to control the systems upon which our future depends.

We commit ourselves never to regard our descendants as mere heirs to risks we refused to assume.

We will remember that technology is a means, that the human being is an end, and that progress has meaning only if it remains at the service of humanity.

**Such is the commitment we propose to our generation.**

---

# Conclusion

## What We Must Pass On

One day perhaps, our descendants will look upon our era as we today look upon the first humans who discovered fire.

They will not ask only:

**“What did they know?”**

They will ask above all:

**“What did they choose to do with their power?”**

They will know that we created artificial intelligences capable of learning, calculating, creating, and perhaps surpassing human beings in many fields; but above all, they will seek to know whether we succeeded in remaining human.

For here is the paradox that could define our century:

> **We risk building ever more intelligent machines in a civilization that could become ever less wise.**

We must therefore not fear intelligence, but **intelligence without a compass**; we must not fear power, but **power without Goodness**; we must not fear the machine, but the day when human beings ask it to become their master.

The true question may not be:

**“Will AI become more intelligent than humanity?”**

It will be:

> **“Will humanity know how to remain human when an artificial intelligence is more powerful than it is?”**

If we answer yes, AI could become one of the greatest instruments of service ever conceived: it could relieve suffering, accelerate knowledge, help the most vulnerable, broaden access to knowledge, augment human capabilities, and free up time for education, creation, research, and human relationships.

If we answer no, it could become the gigantic mirror of our own disorders: our greed, our violence, our lies, our divisions, our addictions, our impulses, and our will to power.

An artificial intelligence will not save a civilization that no longer knows what it wants to save.

It will not give meaning to a society that has lost the meaning of the Good.

It will replace neither conscience, nor love, nor responsibility, nor freedom.

And above all, it will never replace what gives human existence its value.

Human beings can build calculation; they cannot manufacture truth.

They can manufacture a simulation of conversation; they cannot manufacture love.

They can create an intelligence; they cannot, through increased computing power alone, create the ultimate meaning of existence.

They may even build a god of silicon.

**But they must never surrender their humanity to it.**

As machines become more powerful, humanity must become wiser.

As algorithms become more complex, human beings must become more responsible.

As artificial intelligences become more autonomous, humanity must defend its freedom more strongly.

And when the power of our creations seems to exceed our imagination, we must return to what is older than all our machines: **Goodness.**

It reminds intelligence that it must serve, power that it must protect, science that it must seek truth, the economy that it must contribute to the Common Good, technology that it must remain an instrument, and human beings that they must never turn their own creations into masters.

The future of humanity will therefore not be determined solely in artificial intelligence laboratories.

It will be decided by a much older question: **What will we do with our freedom?**

For the day humanity possesses an artificial intelligence capable of solving almost every problem, one question will remain that no machine can answer in its place:

> **“What must we love, protect, and pass on?”**

If we lose this compass, we may perhaps build the most intelligent world in all of history…

**and become the least wise beings within it.**

The true project of civilization is therefore not to build a machine that becomes our master.

It is to build a civilization wise enough that, even in the face of an intelligence superior to our own, **human beings remain at the service of the Good, Truth, freedom, dignity, and the Common Good.**

AI can become our servant. **It must never become our idol.**

---

# Humanity's Duty

# **Intelligence can give us power. Science can give us knowledge. Technology can give us the means. But no machine will ever tell us what we must love, protect, and pass on. That responsibility will remain humanity's.**

# **Our first duty will be never to sacrifice the human being to the power of what humanity has created.**

# Public Status of the Manifesto

This manifesto is conceived as a **public-interest resource**.

It belongs to no party, no religion, no company, no state, and no organization.

It requires no particular religious, political, or philosophical allegiance.

It may be read, copied, shared, translated, printed, taught, discussed, criticized, adapted, improved, and integrated into other projects.

Its purpose is not to create a new authority, but to help create a **common compass for humanity in the age of artificial intelligence**.

No generation possesses, by itself, the wisdom necessary to foresee all the consequences of AI.

This manifesto must therefore remain open to criticism and improvement.

The only requirement that must never be abandoned is its founding principle:

> **Technological power must remain at the service of human dignity, freedom, truth, Goodness, and the Common Good.**

## **A free text for a free humanity.**
